### Notes for the reviewer
- No new dependency. `ThemeSwitch.test.tsx` needed no mock change.
- Duration and easing are read from `--dur-theme` and `--ease-in-out`. `Number.parseFloat` turns `500ms` into 500. The duration test stubs `320ms` and `cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.2, 1)`, so a hardcoded 500 or curve would fail. The `0px` in the clip-path strings is the fence's circle syntax; `guard:design` does not scan TypeScript for px.
- The clip uses `fill: "both"` so the circle stays open until the snapshot goes away. The tests assert centre, radius, duration, easing and `pseudoElement`.
- A press whose painted theme does not change calls `chooseTheme` and returns before the running reveal is skipped. That is the fence's first step. A later press that does change the painted theme cancels the clip, then `skipTransition()`, then starts its own. A serial drops a `ready` handler that belongs to an older press.
- Web Interface Guidelines, where the design contract wins: the reveal animates `clip-path` and, without the API, colour, which `docs/DESIGN_SYSTEM.md` §7 names beyond `transform` and `opacity`. Reduced motion is instant in script (no `data-theme-fade`, no `startViewTransition`), which is the fence and §7; the guideline's "keep a colour transition" does not apply. The fade lists properties rather than `transition: all`. Buttons stay `type="button"` with their existing `aria-label` and `:focus-visible` ring. The handler does not move focus. `getBoundingClientRect` runs in the press handler, not during render.
- Impeccable `animate.md` would keep a colour transition under reduced motion. `docs/DESIGN_SYSTEM.md` §7 and the fence require an instant change. The fence wins.
- OWASP XSS sheet: the reveal writes no HTML. The clip path is computed from the button box and the viewport.
